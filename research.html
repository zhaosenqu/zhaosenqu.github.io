Current

Metal-organic framework / TMD heterostructures

Kempa Group, Department of Chemistry, Johns Hopkins University · PI: Prof. Thomas J. Kempa · Aug 2026 – present

Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) offer lattices whose geometry and chemistry can be tuned by design. This project asks whether a MOF placed on an atomically thin transition-metal dichalcogenide (TMD) can imprint a periodic potential on the semiconductor beneath it, offering a chemical route to engineering 2D electronic structure.

Approach

  • Synthesize bulk and few-layer MOFs by chemical vapor deposition (CVD).
  • Transfer MOFs onto monolayer TMDs to form 2D MOF–TMD heterostructures.
  • Characterize morphology and optical response of the MOF, TMD, and heterostructure using AFM and optical spectroscopy.

Methods

CVD · 2D materials transfer · AFM · optical spectroscopy

2026

Nitrided buffer layers for low-temperature sputtered AlN

Center for Nanophase Materials Sciences, Oak Ridge National Laboratory · DOE ORISE · PI: Dr. Kyle P. Kelley · May – Aug 2026

Aluminum nitride is a wide-bandgap piezoelectric and photonic material, but high-quality films usually require high growth temperatures or single-crystal substrates. This project developed an oxynitride interlayer on plasma-treated SiO2 so that reactively sputtered AlN grows with lower mosaicity at 350 °C, a thermal budget compatible with CMOS back-end processing and scalable photonic integration.

Approach

  • Varied oxynitride interlayer treatment conditions for sputtered AlN on SiO2, using c-plane sapphire as a control substrate.
  • Used XRD, AFM, and Raman spectroscopy as feedback on film mosaicity, crystallinity, and surface roughness.

Outcome

  • Optimized oxynitride treatment improved the structural quality of AlN films on SiO2.
  • Co-first-author manuscript in preparation for APL Materials; poster at the DOE ORISE Summer 2026 intern session. Publications

Methods

Reactive magnetron sputtering · plasma surface treatment · XRD · AFM · Raman spectroscopy

2024 – 2026

Structure–property relationships in sputtered Cu and Cu–Ti alloys

Weihs Group, Department of Materials Science & Engineering, Johns Hopkins University · PI: Prof. Timothy P. Weihs · Dec 2024 – May 2026

Metals used as conductors face a trade-off between mechanical strength and electrical conductivity. This work examined how grain morphology, crystallographic texture, and defect density in sputter-deposited, freestanding Cu, Cu–Ti, and Ti–V foils govern hardness and conductivity.

Approach

  • Quantified grain morphology and texture by SEM and electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD).
  • Assessed defect structure by XRD and correlated it with Vickers microhardness to identify strengthening mechanisms.

Outcome

  • Demonstrated composition-dependent precipitation strengthening in freestanding Cu–Ti foils, reaching a peak hardness of about 350 HV; intermediate Ti contents gave the best hardness–conductivity balance.
  • Co-author on a published article and a manuscript under review in Materials Science and Engineering: A. Publications

Methods

SEM · EBSD · XRD · Vickers microhardness

2025

Scaling AlScN ferroelectric diode memory

Department of Electrical & Systems Engineering, University of Pennsylvania · NSF Singh REU · PI: Prof. Deep M. Jariwala · May – Aug 2025

Ferroelectric aluminum scandium nitride (AlScN) is a CMOS-compatible candidate for dense non-volatile memory. This project scaled AlScN ferroelectric diodes from 50 µm down to the 40 nm node and examined what limits switching and reliability at small dimensions.

Approach

  • Fabricated and scaled AlScN diode memory devices from 50 µm to 40 nm.
  • Characterized ferroelectric switching in sub-50 nm diodes with P–E hysteresis and I–V measurements.
  • Investigated 5 nm AlN / 15 nm AlScN heterostructures to study interfacial coupling through proximity ferroelectricity.

Outcome

  • Demonstrated viability for high-density storage of 100 Mbit/mm2.
  • ITO/Al top-contact engineering reduced the coercive field by 20% and leakage current by 10×.
  • AlN/AlScN stacks showed interfacial coupling that enhances polarization, retention, and device reliability.
  • Co-author on the resulting Nano Letters article; presented at the Singh REU Symposium (2025) and the Yale Undergraduate Research Conference (2026). Publications

Methods

Electron-beam lithography · thin-film deposition · reactive ion etching · lift-off · Keithley SMU P–E and I–V measurement
