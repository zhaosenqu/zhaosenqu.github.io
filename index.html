01 / Selected research

Research at the materials–device interface

My work connects synthesis and processing decisions to measurable device behavior. These projects best represent the questions I want to pursue in doctoral research.

R.01 Current
2D electronic materials · Johns Hopkins

MOF–TMD heterostructures as tunable periodic potentials

Can a chemically programmable metal-organic framework impose a useful periodic potential on an atomically thin semiconductor?

My contribution
Synthesize bulk and few-layer MOFs by CVD, transfer them onto monolayer TMDs, and evaluate heterostructure morphology and optical response.
Methods
Chemical vapor deposition, layer transfer, AFM, optical spectroscopy
Research lead
Prof. Thomas J. Kempa · Aug 2026–present
R.02Penn REU
Ferroelectric devices · University of Pennsylvania

Highly scaled AlScN ferroelectric diode memory

Scaled non-volatile AlScN diodes from 200 µm to the 40 nm node and investigated how contact design and heterostructure interfaces shape switching, leakage, and reliability.

My contribution
Fabricated sub-50 nm devices and characterized P–E hysteresis and I–V behavior; studied ITO/Al top contacts and 5 nm AlN / 15 nm AlScN stacks.
Result
20% lower coercive field and 10× lower leakage current through contact engineering; work contributed to a Nano Letters article.
Methods
E-beam lithography, photolithography, PVD, RIE, lift-off, electrical characterization
R.03DOE ORISE
Nitride thin films · Oak Ridge National Laboratory

Low-temperature AlN process integration

Studied nitrided interlayers for reactively sputtered AlN on plasma-treated SiO2, using sapphire controls to separate substrate and process effects.

My contribution
Varied interlayer and deposition conditions, then used structural and surface measurements as feedback to evaluate film texture, crystallinity, and roughness.
Significance
A 350 °C sputtering route supports lower-thermal-budget, CMOS-compatible photonic integration.
Methods
Reactive magnetron sputtering, XRD, AFM, Raman spectroscopy
