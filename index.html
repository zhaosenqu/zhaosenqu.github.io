About

I am a sophomore at Johns Hopkins University pursuing a B.S. in Electrical Engineering and Materials Science & Engineering & Engineering (GPA: 3.98 / 4.00), with minors in Mathematics and Energy. My research sits at the intersection of semiconductor device physics, nanofabrication, and advanced materials characterization.

My work spans ferroelectric memory scaling, copper thin-film metallurgy, and next-generation nitride photonics. I have fabricated devices in Class 100 cleanroom environments and characterized materials using SEM/EBSD, XRD, and AFM. This summer I will join the Center for Nanophase Materials Sciences at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) to grow doped AlN thin films for integrated photonic and electronic applications.

Outside the lab, I serve as a teaching assistant for Linear Algebra and Intermediate Programming, and I enjoy mentoring fellow undergraduates.

NSF REU Nanofabrication Research Intern

Device Research and Engineering Laboratory  ·  University of Pennsylvania

Philadelphia, PA  ·  PI: Prof. Deep Jariwala

May – Aug 2025

Scaled ferroelectric diode non-volatile memory devices from 200 µm down to 40 nm — a 5,000× reduction — in a Class 100 cleanroom. Fabricated devices using physical vapor deposition (PVD), photolithography, and reactive ion etching (RIE), achieving a 20% reduction in coercive field and 10× decrease in leakage current. Work resulted in a co-authored publication in Nano Letters.
