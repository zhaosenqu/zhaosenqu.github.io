About

I am a senior at Johns Hopkins University pursuing a B.S. in Electrical Engineering with a minor in Mathematics, and I am applying to Electrical Engineering PhD programs for Fall 2027. My research sits at the intersection of semiconductor device physics, nanofabrication, and thin-film synthesis—building electronic and photonic devices from the material up and scaling them toward manufacturable technology.

At the University of Pennsylvania I scaled ferroelectric AlScN non-volatile memory devices from 200 µm to the 40 nm node, cutting coercive field by 20% and leakage current 10× through contact engineering—work that contributed to a publication in Nano Letters. At Oak Ridge National Laboratory (DOE ORISE) I optimized oxynitride buffer layers for low-temperature, CMOS-compatible AlN thin films, now the subject of a co-first-authored manuscript in APL Materials. My cleanroom experience spans photolithography, e-beam lithography, reactive sputtering, CVD, and RIE.

I currently work in the Kempa Lab at Johns Hopkins, synthesizing 2D metal-organic-framework–TMD heterostructures to engineer periodic potentials in atomically thin semiconductors. I am most drawn to problems that demand tight integration of materials design, device physics, and process engineering—and I intend to pursue them through a PhD and a research career in semiconductor R&D. Outside the lab, I serve as a head course assistant and teaching assistant, and I enjoy mentoring fellow undergraduates.

DOE ORISE Research Student Intern

Center for Nanophase Materials Sciences  ·  Oak Ridge National Laboratory

Oak Ridge, TN  ·  PI: Dr. Kyle P. Kelley

May – Aug 2026

Optimized oxynitride interlayers on reactively sputtered aluminum nitride (AlN) thin films (on SiO2, with c-axis sapphire controls), tuning deposition conditions to maximize film mosaicity. Characterized mosaicity, crystallinity, and surface roughness by XRD, AFM, and Raman spectroscopy, demonstrating improved structural quality with optimized oxynitride treatment. This low-temperature (350 °C) sputtering route enables more direct, CMOS-compatible photonic integration at scale. Work is the subject of a co-first-authored manuscript in APL Materials.

Device Research and Engineering Laboratory  ·  University of Pennsylvania

Philadelphia, PA  ·  PI: Prof. Deep M. Jariwala

May – Aug 2025

Scaled ferroelectric AlScN diode non-volatile memory devices from 200 µm to the 40 nm node, demonstrating viability for high-density (100 Mbit/mm²) storage.
