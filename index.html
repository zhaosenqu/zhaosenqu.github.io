About

I am a sophomore at Johns Hopkins University pursuing a dual B.S. in Electrical Engineering and Materials Science & Engineering , with minors in Mathematics and Energy. My research focuses on the intersection of semiconductor device physics, nanofabrication, and advanced materials characterization—work that bridges fundamental science and scalable engineering.

I have scaled ferroelectric AlScN memory devices from 200 µm to the 40 nm node, contributed to studies on copper thin-film metallurgy, and co-authored papers in Nano Letters and Materials Science and Engineering: A. My cleanroom experience spans photolithography, e-beam lithography, PVD, and RIE at both the University of Pennsylvania and Johns Hopkins. This summer I will join the Center for Nanophase Materials Sciences at Oak Ridge National Laboratory to grow doped AlN thin films for next-generation photonic and electronic applications.

I am driven by problems that require tight integration of materials design, device physics, and process engineering—whether that leads to a PhD or an advanced R&D role in industry. Outside the lab, I serve as a teaching assistant for Linear Algebra and Intermediate Programming and enjoy mentoring fellow undergraduates.

NSF REU Nanofabrication Research Intern

Device Research and Engineering Laboratory  ·  University of Pennsylvania

Philadelphia, PA  ·  PI: Prof. Deep Jariwala

May – Aug 2025

Scaled ferroelectric AlScN diode non-volatile memory devices from 200 µm to the 40 nm node, demonstrating viability for high-density (100 Mbit/mm²) storage. Fabricated devices in a Class 100 cleanroom using PVD, electron-beam lithography, photolithography, and RIE; developed lift-off processes for sub-50 nm features. Achieved a 20% reduction in coercive field and 10× decrease in leakage current through top-contact engineering. Also investigated proximity ferroelectricity in 5 nm AlN / 15 nm AlScN stacks. Results contributed to a co-authored publication in Nano Letters.
